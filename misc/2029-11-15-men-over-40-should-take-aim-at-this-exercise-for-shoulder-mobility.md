# Men Over 40 Should Take Aim at This Exercise for Shoulder Mobility

Trainer, author, and fitness model Kirk Charles, NASM-CPT CES, knows that as you get older, life can get more complicated. But that shouldn’t prevent you from being on top of your game. He’ll help to answer the tough training questions that come with age so you too can be Fit Beyond 40.

When you get older, nagging aches that you'd shrug off in your younger years can become bigger issues. A few of my clients have experienced this with shoulder pain—and the typical solution is rest, and sometimes even physical therapy work. For clients who really struggle with R&R, there are options to keep moving. I had one particular client who wasn't willing to stop training completely—so my response was for him to try an exercise that could help promote mobility and strength, the archer row. The movement was an easy way for him to work his shoulder stabilizer muscles without moving a lot of weight.

[Take Aim](https://pigmentlab.jcesqc8.com/)

To get started, pick up a light exercise band. Get into a half-kneeling position with your right knee forward. Hold the ends of the resistance band in each hand with your right arm slightly above shoulder height and fully extended. Keep your left elbow bent with your left hand holding the band about even with your right elbow. Keep both thumbs pointing upward toward the ceiling—this will allow you to keep your shoulders in external rotation.

From this starting position, pull back with your left hand while keeping the band close to your chest (as if you’re pulling the string of a bow to shoot an arrow). Make sure your right arm stays straight the whole time as you’re pulling with your left hand. Pull as far back as you can, then pause and squeeze for a count. Then release your left hand and arm back to the starting position. That’s one rep. If you don't feel like you're putting forth any effort, you may need to double up the band or choose a different one for more resistance.

One of the great aspects of the archer row is that it works your back, particularly your rhomboids, along with your shoulders. This is true for both parts of the movement—your straight arm is in an isometric position working your rotator cuff and rhomboids, while your other arm is working the same muscles as you pull. The half-kneeling position also allows you to lock in your core.

For my particularly stubborn client, we started out by lowering his right arm just a little bit below shoulder height. After more than a month, as his mobility improved, he was finally able to lift above shoulder height. To this day he still does the exercise several times a week as a warm up for other activities.
