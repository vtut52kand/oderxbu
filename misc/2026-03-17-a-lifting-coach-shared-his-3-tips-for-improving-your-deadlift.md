In a recent video, fitness coach and YouTuber Eugene Teo demonstrated a series of small adjustments that beginners can make in their deadlift technique in order to be able to pull more safely, and build a strong foundation.

Shoulder and shoulder blade position

Teo explains that leaning with your shoulders positioned vertically over the bar with your shoulder blades behind it as you prepare to lift can cause you to drop your hips, and then wrench up through your back. Meanwhile, leaning too far forward over the bar will lead you to drive more of that lift through your lower back. The shoulder positioning "sweet spot" will have your shoulder blades directly over the bar, and your shoulders slightly in front of the barbell.

[Your Deadlift](https://metabolic-horizon.bbsxyxy.com/)

"What you'll find here is that your hips will naturally be positioned not too high and not too low," he explains. "From here, the deadlift itself becomes a relatively simple movement. All you're doing is just standing up. Don't think too much about squeezing anything, or thrusting anything, or pulling back. Just stand up."

Stance

Teo believes that if your shoulder position is correct, then your body will naturally realign itself to the most comfortable stance for the lift. "If I'm too far back, and my shoulders are positioned over the barbell, when I go to pull you'll see how I'm going to start falling forwards, so I'll naturally want to step in closer. If I'm positioned in too close, my legs will naturally push the barbell into a better position before I start the lift."

Teo concludes by outlining the ideal form you should be exhibiting throughout each stage of the lift. First, ensure your pelvis is stacked vertically under your ribcage. Take a deep breath in through your diaphragm, and you should feel your midsection inflate as you do this. Next, reach your arms as far down your legs as possible to engage the lats, making sure not to hunch over. Slowly push your hips back to bend down and grab the barbell, maintaining that stack of the pelvis and the ribcage. Grab onto the barbell, grip it tightly, pull the slack out of it, and then stand up, and then lower the weight back down in a slow, controlled manner.

"Spend a few workouts just getting into your groove with light deadlifts," he advises. "Aim for sets of maybe 5 or 10 reps, and make sure every single one of those reps looks exactly the same with a comfortable weight. Once you can do this and you've ingrained this movement pattern with that sweet spot, you can start increasing the weights and thinking about total body tension."
