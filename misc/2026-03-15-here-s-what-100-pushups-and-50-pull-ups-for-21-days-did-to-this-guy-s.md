# Here's What 100 Pushups and 50 Pull-Ups for 21 Days Did to This Guy's Body

If you are already a regular gymgoer, how much can doing 100 push-ups and 50 pull-ups every day for three weeks really change your body? Turns out, actually quite a bit.

Fraser Young of 9to5Strength recently took on an intense upper body regimen as an experiment, and documented his results on YouTube. Young pulled the exercise from a 2009 Testosterone Nation article, which claimed such a regimen could add two inches to your chest measurement in three weeks. With that goal in mind, he committed to doing 100 push-ups and 50 pull-ups for 21 days. That’s a whopping 1,800 push-ups and 800 pull-ups in less than a month.

The article advises to only do pull-ups and push-ups as upper body exercises for the duration of the program. It also suggests completing reps as fast as possible, but never doing reps until failure. In Young’s case, he did 6-8 pulls ups, took a 2 minute rest, completed 10-15 pull ups, then completed 6-8 pull-ups during another rest period. After that, he repeated the cycle.

Before attempting the program, Young weighed in at 157 pounds with a 40.25” chest and 32” waist. After completing the program, he weighed in at 161 pounds with a 41.37” chest and 32.25” waist. That’s a little over an inch added to Young’s chest—and you can see a difference.
