Understanding the Medical Evaluation

The evaluation process will be heavily informed by your symptoms and reasoning for seeking testosterone therapy. As long as there are no life-threatening symptoms present, a mixture of moderate and common low testosterone markers would likely lead to blood tests.

Blood panels can often include a comprehensive work up of many diagnostics, however, the primary focus would be for testosterone serum levels. A measurement taken when your levels are at their highest (ie. in the morning shortly after waking up) will show your physician the maximum testosterone circulating in your body.

Generally, low blood testosterone is characterized by levels lower than 250 ng/dL, and blood tests will likely be conducted to measure your luteinizing hormone (LH) and prolactin levels. Both of these hormones are linked to the regulation of sex hormones and can provide additional insight into the contributing causes of your symptoms. Coupling your symptoms, a physical exam, and the results of your blood panel will help your doctor come to an appropriate diagnosis.
