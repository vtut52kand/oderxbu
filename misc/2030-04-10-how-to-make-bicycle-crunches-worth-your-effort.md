# How to Make Bicycle Crunches Worth Your Effort

[Worth Your](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/8617626532.html)

So you're bored with crunches and situps. Fine, that's not a huge surprise. Those exercises are basic, and they take a focus to do properly that lots of guys just aren't willing to give. But you might want to include some type of spinal flexion in your core routine to target your abs, and if you can also add some rotation into the mix, you'll be in even better shape.

One go-to variation that most people work up to are bicycle crunches, which get your legs involved with movement that looks fairly close to pedaling an invisible bike on your back, especially if you crank up the speed.

But the exercise isn't something that you should think of as a sprint. Slow and steady will serve you much better. Just take it from Men's Health fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S.

"The bicycle crunch is a terrifically useful core move that lets you train your abs through rotation, but the objective isn’t to run through the bicycle motion at light speed," he says. "Doing so causes you to lose tension through your core and abs, and winds up defeating the purpose of the movement altogether."

Use Samuel's three-step checklist to make sure you're doing the move the right way.

Start at Work

Eb says: First, think of the starting position not as a position of rest, but as one of work. Essentially, you’re thinking of starting from a hollow hold (and I sometimes teach the bicycle crunch with this as the start position). This mental approach insures that your lower back is always pressed into the ground and your abs are constantly working, even between reps.

You could do sloppy bicycle crunches all day, but aiming to start in a hollow hold will have your abs burning after barely a minute. Return to the hollow hold after each rep, instead of lying on the ground like a fish.

Move With Intention

Eb says: Second, take your time. When you crunch up and opposite knee and elbow touch, try to pause there for a split second, forcing your abs to continue to flex through rotation. This insures that you’re not relying on momentum to propel you and also challenges your abs to be in control of the entire motion.

Eb says: Third, keep your neck relaxed. Like all crunches, one of the shortcomings of the bicycle crunch is how convenient it is for you to shift your neck into cervical flexion (leading to a sore neck later).

We want this motion coming from our abs, not our neck, so imagine a tennis ball or apple between your chin and neck. Maintain that space for the life of each set.

One final note: Forget counting reps. Try starting out by working for 3 to 4 sets of 40 seconds on, 20 off.
