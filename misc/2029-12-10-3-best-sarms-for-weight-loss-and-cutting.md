What are SARMs?

The human body has Androgen receptors throughout, spread from muscles, and bones, to even the prostate.

They play a crucial role, as the natural Androgenic hormones humans produce need to bind at specific places for our bodies to function normally.

Thus, a good and safe amount of Androgenic hormones is all fine and good.

Steroids are exogenous Androgenic hormones. These bind to all of the androgenic receptors in the human body and cause a biological change in them.

This is great for muscle hypertrophy, but not so great for other parts – such as your prostate or your liver.

See, those will also undergo the change due to the exogenous hormones, and that’s how you end up with prostate cancer.

Steroids are often prescribed to individuals suffering from certain muscular atrophy illnesses, such as AIDS patients. In fact, AIDS patients are often prescribed Anadrol.

Anadrol is an extremely powerful oral steroid, and incredibly liver toxic. SARMs, like the RAD140 shown above, were designed to perform similarly to steroids in muscular hypertrophy but to not have the adverse effects associated with steroids.

SARMs, as the name suggests, are selective in nature, and will only (mostly) bind to the Androgen receptors within the muscles and bones, therefore having fewer side effects than steroids.

Are SARMs Legal?

So, are SARMs legal? It’s a bit of a gray area. The key is in the labeling – they must be marketed as “Research Chemicals” for legal purchase. Stray away from this designation, and you’re venturing into illegal territory.

For athletes in sports regulated by the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA), the use of SARMs poses a challenge. Unfortunately, luck isn’t on their side, as these compounds are firmly banned in such competitive arenas.

Now, let’s delve a bit deeper into the legal nuances. The legality of SARMs lies in their classification as “Research Chemicals,” a label that grants them a legal status, at least in the context of purchase and possession.

However, the intricacies don’t end there. Athletes, in particular, need to tread carefully. WADA, the authoritative body overseeing anti-doping measures, has cast a wide net, capturing not just performance-enhancing substances but also caffeine.

Yes, that’s right – even something as innocent as consuming too much coffee before a test can lead to a ban.

This legal puzzle surrounding SARMs reflects the broader landscape of substances straddling the line between research and illicit use.

It emphasizes the need for users to be well-versed in the regulatory intricacies surrounding these compounds.

The blurred boundaries of legality underscore the importance of responsible and informed decision-making, especially for those engaged in competitive sports where adherence to anti-doping regulations is paramount.

Side Effects of SARMs

Regrettably, every rose has its thorn. Indulging in chips may bring guilt, exploring online dating may bring unexpected outcomes, and cruising in a Tesla may evoke a sense of smugness.

Similarly, the use of SARMs is not without its consequences, and these effects tend to be intricately tied to the dosage. In simple terms, the more you use, the more pronounced the side effects become.

The manifestation of side effects can vary widely, influenced by individual factors and the specific type of SARM in play.

Let’s take a closer look at some of the most prevalent and noteworthy side effects associated with these compounds.

1) Testosterone Suppression

Yup, even SARMs can do this. See, normally exogenous Testosterone will suppress your endogenous production of Testosterone.

SARMs, most of them at least, do not cause this at all. Some SARMs such as the aforementioned S4 can do this.

In this scenario, it might be best to make use of a PCT (post-cycle treatment) to keep your testes functioning.

2) Organ Damage

While SARMs were designed to not be as toxic as steroids, they unfortunately are still toxic, just like your ex. Specifically the liver and kidneys.

See, all oral steroids and SARMs need to be broken down by the liver, and that process does put a fair bit of strain on the liver. Solution? Lower doses, or just use the correct supplements.

3) Cholesterol

Another common side effect we hear steroid users complain about is, “Oh, my lipids are out of range.”. Basically, they have cholesterol issues.

This is something that SARMs definitely do cause, as quite a few studies (and anecdotal evidence) do show that SARMs lower HDL (the good cholesterol).
