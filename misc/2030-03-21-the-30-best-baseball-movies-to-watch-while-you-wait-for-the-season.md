# The 30 Best Baseball Movies to Watch While You Wait for the Season

Our product picks are editor-tested, expert-approved. We may earn a commission through links on our site. Why Trust Us?

No one could've expected that America's pastime would be sidelined by a worldwide pandemic. But alas, that's what's happened, curtailing the start of the MLB's 2020 season. And while baseball is coming back for a shorter season, players and fans are still concerned for how things will shape up to be. Until that happens, there's still a yearning for some good, old-fashioned baseball.

In a way, baseball and acting are two peas in a pod. Feel-good stories of those who might never make it whose dreams suddenly come true. Fame, fortune, glory—it's embedded into both, and together can create magic for some beautiful filmmaking that will leave you nostalgic for the days on the diamond or in the stadium.

If you're looking for a heroic final at-bat, or a tale of underdogs defeating the sure to win team, then we have a list of the best movies to watch when you're missing the game. And after going through this list, keep an eye out for the newest film in the baseball genre—ESPN's documentary about the unforgettable 1998 home run chase between Mark McGwire and Sammy Sosa.

Field of Dreams (1989)

Kevin Costner has starred in four different baseball films, but Field of Dreams is without a doubt his most famous one. The story of an Iowa farmer who mysteriously hears a corn field tell him, "If you build it, he will come" has become part of the American lexicon, and that's just one of the many reasons you should watch it.

Moneyball (2011)

[Watch While You Wait](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/)

So there might not be a ton of baseball played throughout the movie. But that's the whole point—Moneyball shows you that there's more going on than just what happens on the field. This adaptation of Michael Lewis' acclaimed book perfectly captures the culture clash between the analytics crowd and the on-field crowd, and Brad Pitt perfectly plays the Oakland Athletics' real life GM, Billy Beane. Toss in one of Jonah Hill's best roles (both he and Pitt were nominated for an Oscar), an Aaron Sorkin-penned script, and the true underdog story of the 2002 Oakland A's, and there's no way you can't watch this.

A League of Their Own (1992)

An all-star female cast (including Geena Davis, Rosie O'Donnell and Madonna) stars in this classic. Based on the true story of the All-American Girls Professional Baseball League, which replaced the MLB throughout WWII, Tom Hanks (as the team's manager) completes the film with this iconic line: "There's no crying in baseball!"

42 (2013)

The 2013 film stars Chadwick Boseman (pre-Black Panther) as Jackie Robinson, telling the story of the famed baseball player's journey to breaking the racial barrier in the MLB. The movie's keen cinematography and stellar performances make the film feel more powerful than ever given current world events and protests.

The Sandlot (1993)

The movie that every kid has seen has probably impacted the way most people between the ages of 20 and 45 think about baseball. The heartwarming story of a young boy learning about life and the game from a group of neighborhood kids holds dear to many hearts and continues to be a classic.

The Bad News Bears (1976)

If you haven't seen this movie yet, then what are you doing reading this list? The iconic film tells the story about a curmudgeon ex-minor league baseball player who finds himself coaching a misfit little league team. Be prepared—this might just become one of your favorite comedies ever.

Undrafted (2016)

Newer to the baseball film genre, the flick (based on a true story) follows a summer-league baseball team playing an important game when their star player finds out that he wasn't drafted to the Major Leagues. To put it short—it's essentially an updated version of Bad News Bears.

Rookie of the Year (1993)

How does a kid who winds up with a wicked arm after a freak accident somehow assist the struggling Chicago Cubs? That's the story of this film, something of a companion to Angels in the Outfield.

The Perfect Game (2009)

In baseball, the Little League World Series has similar glory to the MLB's World Series. This film explores the events leading up to the 1957 Little League World Series, with the first team outside of the U.S. to win the entire thing. And cool fact—the pitcher of the Mexican team that won is still the only person to ever throw a perfect game in the championship.

61 (2001)

The Billy Crystal-directed HBO film tells the story of Mickey Mantle and Roger Maris racing to beat Babe Ruth's historic home run record in 1961. The tale is legendary and the layered performances are a knock out of the park—pun very much intended.

Bull Durham (1988)

Another Kevin Costner baseball film! The flick follows a minor league catcher who gets sent to the Single-A Durham Bulls to mentor a young pitcher with promise. An ego clash, a love triangle, some damn good baseball... where can you go wrong?

Major League (1989)

When the owner of the Cleveland Indians decides to make the team exceptionally bad in order to move them to Miami, no one expects the dream team that forms as a result. Among notable cast members? Charlie Sheen as the team's x factor, a bespectacled relief pitcher named Ricky "Wild Thing" Vaughn.

Million Dollar Arm (2014)

Based on the true story of Rinku Singh and Dinesh Patel being signed to the Pittsburgh Pirates, the 2014 film was produced by ESPN and Bill Simmons—notable names for any sports fans. And if you also happen to be a Mad Men fan, this is really one of you—Jon Hamm plays the lead role.

Sugar (2008)

Baseball doesn't just have a home in the United Staes—the sport has the power to span all cultures, as this film explores. Telling the story of a Dominican pitcher who lands a chance to play in the U.S. minor leagues, it's a heartwarming story that shows the emotional pursuit of making dreams come true.

Trouble With the Curve (2012)

In Clint Eastwood's first time acting after 2008's Gran Torino, Trouble With The Curve follows a baseball scout whose daughter comes along with him for a scouting trip. The film's star-studded cast features Amy Adams and Justin Timberlake—with those names, you know at the very least it'll be watchable.

Fever Pitch (2005)

Okay—this isn't necessarily a baseball movie, and more a romantic comedy. But this film based on Nick Hornsby's book of the same name (though his experiences were with English soccer team Arsenal, and not the Boston Red Sox), definitely shows off how dedicated baseball fans are to their team, often to the point of obsession. Plus you have Jimmy Fallon and Drew Barrymore for the main leads. Charming!

The Natural (1984)

In one of the most memorable baseball films, Robert Redford portrays a player returning sixteen years after his initial chance of playing ball to bring the troubled New York Knights to a win. Although the movie changes the ending of the novel it's based on, no one can forget the iconic scene of Redford's home run with sparks flying through the air... from a broken light.

The Rookie (2002)

Dennis Quaid stars as a high school coach who, after suffering from a shoulder injury during his time in the minor leagues, has his team make a deal with him: if they win the district's championship then he has to try out for the MLB. It's based on a true story, and it shows viewers that it's never too late to achieve your dreams.

The Phenom (2016)

Starring Johnny Simmons and Ethan Hawke, this movie tells the story of a young man whose innate pitching abilities have him attempting to achieve his dreams while trying to resolve his fractured and unstable childhood with his psychologist (Paul Giamatti) while dealing with his father (Hawke).

Bottom of the 9th (2019)

Real-life couple Sofia Vergara and Joe Manganiello star in this film, where the latter goes back to his dreams of being a baseball player after spending some time in prison. It has an all-star cast (including some familiar Sopranos faces).
