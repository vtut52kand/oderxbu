# ‘Euphoria’ Should Give Maude Apatow’s Lexi More To Do

• Through its structure, Euphoria has done a good job of building multi-dimensional lead characters. • Even its side characters, like Maude Apatow's Lexi, have had moments to develop.• Apatow's Lexi deserves more shine in the show's second season.

Sure, Euphoria's first season finale ended with an insane dance/flashback/memory/rabbit hole sequence, scored with an original song from series star Zendaya and Labrinth, who composed much of the show's original music. Sure, there was just as much wild stuff packed in the rest of the episode as the seven weeks preceding. But the best pure moment of the week might've actually—get this—come from the good girl.

That moment was mid-school dance, when the timid Lexi (Maude Apatow), who had just taken several swigs of a Gatorade/Everclear concoction, asked her popular sister, Cassie, how she decides who to hook up with.

"I don't know, they usually just come to you," Cassie told her.

"But what if they don't?" Lexi asked once again, leaned over and slurring her words as a teenager only drinking alcohol for one of the first few times would.

"Then you go for whoever the fuck you want," Cassie again told her.

"Yeah......OK." *long pause* "I'm going to do that," Lexi said.

It was brief scene, but one that nonetheless added a little to a character that has gone much of the show's first season existing in support of other characters, seemingly without a storyline or significant interest of her own. Now, obviously, every character in every show can't have their own subplot. But it's brief moments like the one described above that make side characters feel less like one-note sketches and more like fully-formed people. And it takes a talented performer, like Maude Apatow, who plays Lexi, to pull it off.

This moment played for some really great comedy, but the lightness of the scene didn't last long—instantly, as the rest of the episode did, it cut to another time; Lexi joining Cassie, silently, solemnly, as she went to the clinic for an abortion.

Her appearances in the finale, titled "And Salt the Earth Behind You," were undoubtably brief. A week after playing sounding board to Zendaya's Rue during her big hard-boiled detective/Pepe Silvia rant scene, Lexi's biggest moments came simply sitting in a car, and sitting around a dinner table. As the girls got ready to head to Winter Formal, Maddie hopped into the back seat with a Gatorade bottle filled with a certainly-not-Gatorade fluid. "Fuck it," Lexi says, before taking a swig of Gatorade/Everclear concoction from the bottle. Lexi has served to this point as a 'good-girl' foil to Rue, so seeing her slowly but surely embrace the wild child spectrum has been a great bit of character development.

It's interesting, looking back at the Euphoria's first episode, that Cassie ended up having such a significantly more developed storyline than Lexi did. Of these two sisters, Lexi is introduced first, when Rue comes over and interrupts their dinner, asking Lexi for clean urine so she can pass a drug test. Cassie isn't even shown up close until later in the episode.

Then again, there's the true meaning of the term "supporting actor," or "supporting actress." In a lot of ways, certain categorizations of characters have made us forget what, exactly, 'supporting' really means. For example, when it comes time to nominate Stranger Things, David Harbour has been a shoo-in—he's been nominated in the Best Supporting Actor category for that show's first two seasons already, and likely will be once again for its third. But let's be absolutely clear: he's "supporting" absolutely no one. He's simply a piece of a larger ensemble cast, totally anchoring his own share of the overarching story. Apatow, in Euphoria, is truly supporting: in scenes centered on Rue and Cassie, particularly, she plays off the focus on those characters, typically to great effect.

[Give Maude Apatow Lexi](https://biopeptide.wiki/)

It's worth considering, too, that Apatow came into the show one of its most seasoned stars. Where Angus Cloud (Fez), Hunter Shafer (Jules), and Barbie Ferreira (Kat) were all either brand new or quasi-new to acting, she'd instead had the experience of acting in a couple comedy classics (her father, Judd, is a comedy legend; she appeared in his movies Knocked Up, Funny People, and This is 40, all alongside her mother, actress Leslie Mann, as a child) as well as working with Euphoria creator Sam Levinson in his feature film Assassination Nation. Only a few others—Zendaya most notably—had experience that exceeded that.

Plus, she dressed up like Bob Ross in the Halloween episode. That obviously counts for quite a bit in and of itself, and as a bonus, as she described on The Late Show, the day she was in the costume was the day (note: singular day) that series Executive Producer Drake showed up on set.

With Season 2 on the horizon, Lexi is arguably the most interesting character to have not yet received a focus (though the Fez hive would surely make their case for him as well). We got a bit of her history through Cassie's backstory, but then again, the show surely didn't want to run through everyone all at once. The opportunity to learn more and further flesh out already interesting characters is a great opportunity for Euphoria to continue to tell us who these characters are and what they represent as they go through such a formative time in the youths of their lives.
