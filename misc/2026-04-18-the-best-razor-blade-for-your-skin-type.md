# The Best Razor Blade For Your Skin Type

Buying a razor isn’t rocket science, but your face will benefit if you take the time to make a smart choice about the instrument you push across it several times a week.

Ditch the one-razor-fits-all approach and find one that works for your particular skin type.

Skin Type: Dry

Best Razor to Use: Any

You may have cursed your dry, flaky skin before, but not this time. For a good shave on dry skin, it’s mostly about the prep. When it comes to blades, you’re free to choose whatever type you like best, says Moses Lucero, co-owner of Denver’s Steel + Lather Barber Co.

We like the new Gillette Fusion Proshield, with five blades and a flexible technology that guides blades smoothly along the curves of your face. Bonus: It delivers lubrication before and after each blade to keep your skin ultra-slick.

It’s important to follow up the shave with a heavy-duty moisturizer, like Jack Black Double-Duty Face Moisturizer SPF 20. Lather up with the moisturizer on days you’re not shaving as well, so once you do pull out the razor, your skin won't be as scaly.

Skin Type: Sensitive

Best Razor to Use: Straight Razor

If you’re prone to irritation, go for a straight-razor shave.

Why? Less strokes means less irritation.

Try one from Feather Artist Club, Lucero suggests. The brand offers several blades, but your skin will benefit most from its ProGuard line. “They come with tiny little guards over the blade, and those guards help glide over the prepped skin with minimal pull and tug,” Lucero says.

You cam minimize nicks and cuts with a little extra care. “The key is preparation—especially for sensitive skin,” says Maestro, owner of MaestroShave, a barbershop in Chicago.

[Razor Blade](https://calmvector.dns0663.com/)

Here’s what you should do: Apply a pre-shave oil before and after a shower, then use shaving cream when you grab the razor. The heat of the shower helps the blade glide easily across the skin.

The pre-shave prep should add about 40 seconds to your morning routine—time well-spent if you’re no longer cleaning up nicks and cuts.

Skin Type: Oily

Oily skin is, in a word, easy. “Oily is the most resistant—[guys with it] can use anything,” Lucero says.

The natural oils found in each hair follicle make shaving a breeze. Apply a pre-shave oil after you step out of the shower and the hairs will “come off like butter,” Maestro says.

As for which razor to use, it really comes down to preference—opt for a blade that’s long-lasting, budget-friendly, and easy to replace.

Skin Type: Normal

Best Razor to Use: Electric

If your skin’s not overly sensitive, you can handle a razor with a little power behind it. The major bonus: You’ll be done in no time.

Electric razors are good for guys in a hurry who like a close shave—and ones who shy away from conventional shaves, creams, and balms, Lucero says.

Try Remington F5 Comfort Series Foil Shaver. The blades move side-to-side rather than spinning like rotary blades. This movement prevents the razor from tucking the hair under the skin and causing ingrown hairs or irritation, Lucero says.
