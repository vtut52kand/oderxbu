Palm Springs is a romantic comedy with a Groundhog Day twist. When two singles meet at a wedding, they suddenly find they're stuck in an endless time loop, forced to relive the day over and over again.

Scrooged (1988)

Bill Murray in a comedic take on Charles Dickens' A Christmas Carol. Is that not enough right there? A cynical television executive learns the true spirit of the holidays when, you guessed it, three ghosts of Christmas past, present and future visit to teach him the error of his ways.

The Trip to Greece (2020)

In the final installment of The Trip series, The Trip to Greece features comedians Steve Coogan and Rob Brydon playing exaggerated versions of themselves as they travel together and eat at restaurants across Greece.

Ingrid Goes West (2017)

Social media gets a bit too real in this black comedy. A girl named Ingrid (Aubrey Plaza) becomes obsessed with an influencer (Elizabeth Olsen) and begins to insert herself into the social media star's life.

Bill and Ted Face the Music (2020)

After 20 years, the eponymous duo is back. And this time, they team up with their daughters to help them write a song to save the universe. Time travel can't get any funnier.

Dear White People (2014)

This 2014 dark comedy spawned an equally successful Netflix drama of the same name. Black undergrads at a fictional university navigate school, family and relationships, all while trying to get ahead at their predominantly white institution.

Caddyshack (1980)

The funniest golf movie around, Caddyshack follows a caddie named Danny (Michael O'Keefe), who does everything he can to succeed at his local country club. He just has to work with the outrageous staff and proud golfers.

Beetlejuice (1988)

Say his name three times fast and bam, here he is. When a couple dies in a crash, they realize their spirits are trapped in their home...along with the new homeowners. In comes Beetlejuice (Michael Keaton), a certified "bio-exorcist" who promises to rid the couple of the pesky living humans.

Colossal (2016)

An unemployed writer (Anne Hathaway) realizes when she drinks, she manifests and controls a giant reptilian monster in Seoul. Plus, when her childhood friend (Jason Sudeikis) gets drunk, he manifests into a giant robot in Seoul. Both Hathaway and Sudeikis give great performances to a strange, comedic film.
