What is Drug-Induced Urinary Retention?

This condition occurs when patients have trouble urinating after taking specific medications. Middle-aged men with an enlarged prostate are more susceptible to this condition.

[Drug-Induced Urinary Retention](https://copperglow.dns0663.com/)

Typical Symptoms of Drug-Induced Urinary Retention

Having difficulty urinating or producing less urine

Recently started a new medication, from 3 days to 1 month ago

Often need to urinate again within 2 hours after last going

Need to strain or bear down to pass urine, in the past month

Urine stream is weaker than it used to be

Urine flow stops and starts

Having trouble urinating or need to strain to urinate

Feeling that the bladder is not completely emptied after urination

Treatment of Drug-Induced Urinary Retention

Symptoms should get better after stopping the problematic medication. In severe cases that lead to damage of the kidneys or severe pain, a catheter tube can be inserted to drain the urine and clear the obstruction while the medication wears off.

Q.

Solifenacin Side Effects? Why Your Bladder Is Reacting & Medically Approved Next Steps

A.

Solifenacin can cause anticholinergic side effects because it blocks acetylcholine signals; common issues include dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, and fatigue, and it can occasionally over relax the bladder and lead to urinary retention, with rarer risks like allergic reactions, heat intolerance, and heart rhythm changes. There are several factors to consider. See below to understand more. Medically approved next steps include not stopping the drug suddenly, tracking symptoms, and speaking with your clinician about dose adjustments or alternatives such as beta-3 agonists, pelvic floor therapy, and bladder training, while seeking urgent care right away for inability to urinate, severe abdominal pain, chest symptoms, fainting, or signs of an allergic reaction.
