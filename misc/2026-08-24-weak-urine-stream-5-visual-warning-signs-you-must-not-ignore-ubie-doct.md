A weak urine stream is a slow, dribbling, or interrupted flow of urine that may stop and start several times during urination. It can affect both men and women, though it's more common in men due to prostate-related conditions. Causes range from urinary tract infections and bladder muscle weakness to nerve issues or an enlarged prostate.

Because a weak urine stream can signal anything from a minor infection to a more serious underlying condition, understanding the cause early is key to getting the right care. A weak urine stream can be noticed when the flow of urine is not strong or steady. This might appear as a slow stream that takes longer to empty the bladder.

Signs of a Weak Urine Stream:

Slow Flow: The urine comes out slowly, taking more time than usual.

[Warning Signs You Must](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/blog/3420845476.html)

Dribbling: The urine may dribble out instead of flowing smoothly.

Intermittent Flow: The stream might stop and start several times.

These signs can be linked to conditions like benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), where the prostate gland is enlarged, or bladder outlet obstruction. Both conditions can make it hard for urine to pass through the urethra, leading to a weak stream. If you're experiencing these symptoms, you can check your symptoms with our free AI-powered tool to better understand potential causes. Uroflowmetry, a test that measures the speed and volume of urine flow, is often used to assess these symptoms.
