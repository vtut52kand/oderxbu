# The Right Way to Do a Plank

The fittest people in the world don’t become that way by taking any special supplements, eating a magic superfood, or doing some secret exercises. They simply work harder and put in more hours than anyone else. They’ve also mastered the basics of exercise.

Performing a handful of key exercises with near perfect form each rep allows you to make continual progress. You see a greater return on each rep and you also avoid injury.

That’s why everyone who trains with me at Gym Jones—which is considered one of the world’s best gyms—is taught to master the plank. It’s the simplest, most effective core exercise you can do. It gives you a sold base for your limbs to generate power in all lifts and athletic acts like sprinting or swinging a golf club.

When you do the plank, your goal should be to tense your entire body, like you’re about to take a haymaker. This helps you generate more strength and works your abs harder. Be sure to breathe in this tensed position, so you’ll learn to breathe while resisting load.

[to Do Plank](https://lumina-dermis.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/9552191282.html)

One of my favorite ways to integrate planks is to use them as the “rest” in a workout. It leads to a truly brutal workout, but it also helps you learn to keep your core tight no matter how tired you are, which can help you perform better in long workouts and sports like mixed martial arts, basketball, football, and more.

In one of my favorite workouts, called “Hell Style,” you cycle through a 10-exercise Tabata-style routine where your “rest” is taken holding the most difficult position of each exercise. In it, for example, you do 20 seconds of pushups followed immediately by a 10-second pushup position plank. That’s one round. You do eight rounds, never letting your knees touch the floor, then move onto the next exercises.

“Hell Style” is psychological torture, and it’s about 19 minutes too long. Indeed, it’s my-go to for anyone who needs to not only build a solid, ripped core, but also build mental toughness.
