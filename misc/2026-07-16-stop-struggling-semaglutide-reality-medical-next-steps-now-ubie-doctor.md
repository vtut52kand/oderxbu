Semaglutide is an FDA-approved GLP-1 receptor agonist that helps eligible adults lose weight. In large clinical trials, patients lost an average of 10% to 15% of their body weight when semaglutide was combined with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity.

What Is Semaglutide?

Semaglutide is a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist originally approved for Type 2 diabetes. Under higher doses and a different brand name, it's now FDA-approved specifically for chronic weight management in adults with:

Body mass index (BMI) ≥ 30 kg/m²

BMI ≥ 27 kg/m² plus at least one weight-related condition (e.g., hypertension, type 2 diabetes)

By mimicking a naturally occurring gut hormone, semaglutide helps regulate appetite and food intake.

How Semaglutide Works for Weight Loss

Semaglutide targets key mechanisms that influence eating behavior:

Delays gastric emptying, so you feel full longer

Modulates appetite-centers in the brain, reducing cravings

Stabilizes blood sugar swings, which can curb overeating

Clinical trials report average weight loss of 10–15% of initial body weight over 68 weeks when combined with a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity.

Key Clinical Evidence

Several large, peer-reviewed trials—commonly called the STEP trials—support semaglutide's benefits:

STEP 1 (adults without diabetes): ~15% weight loss vs. ~2.4% with placebo

STEP 2 (adults with type 2 diabetes): ~9.6% vs. ~3.4%

Improvements in blood pressure, cholesterol levels, and glycemic control

These studies, published in leading journals, demonstrate that semaglutide can be a powerful adjunct to lifestyle intervention.

Who Might Benefit Most?

Semaglutide isn't a one-size-fits-all solution. Ideal candidates typically:

Have obesity (BMI ≥ 30) or overweight (BMI ≥ 27) with comorbidities

Have struggled with multiple diet and exercise regimens

Are ready to commit to regular follow-up and lifestyle changes

It's not recommended if you're pregnant, have certain gastrointestinal conditions, or a personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma.

Potential Side Effects & Safety Considerations

Like any medication, semaglutide comes with possible side effects. The most common are gastrointestinal and usually temporary:

Nausea, vomiting

Diarrhea, constipation

Abdominal pain, indigestion

To minimize discomfort, your doctor will start at a low dose and slowly increase over weeks. Rare but serious risks include pancreatitis and gallbladder disease. Always report severe abdominal pain or persistent vomiting.
