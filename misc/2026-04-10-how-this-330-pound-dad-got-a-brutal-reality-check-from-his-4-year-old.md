# How This 330 Pound Dad Got a Brutal Reality Check From His 4 Year Old Son.

Daine Patton, 40, of Lincoln, Nebraska, is the owner of a nuisance wildlife exclusion company, and also works as a mindset and accountability coach for business owners and professionals. His hectic schedule made it easy to overeat and skip exercise, landing him with a weight gain that left him weighing more than 300 pounds. Here, he recounts how his family inspired him to improve his health and mindset for good.

MY WEIGHT GAIN happened slowly after many years of not prioritizing myself. I worked long days, and often ate fast food on the go. I wasn’t exercising regularly, and I leaned too much on comfort food and alcohol when I was stressed. I was eating large portions. Looking back, it was really my lifestyle as a whole that led to the weight gain.

[Pound Dad Got Brutal](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/blog/9747959923.html)

[Got Brutal Reality](https://aegispeptide.snaketongueofficial.com/)

I was at my heaviest between the ages of 32 and 35. At my peak, I weighed around 330 pounds. I felt tired, sluggish, and honestly, disappointed with myself. I used lots of self-deprecating humor, because I knew I wasn’t living anywhere close to my potential. I didn’t feel like the man, father, or husband I wanted to be. Even simple things felt hard. I couldn’t bend over to tie my shoes, or climb stairs with a laundry basket. Physically I felt slow. Mentally I felt stuck.

Then came the brutal push I needed. One day, my four year-old son, Franklin, filled out an “About My Dad” worksheet at his daycare. On it, he wrote that I weighed 400 pounds. And my favorite thing to do? Chug beer. Kids don’t sugarcoat anything.

[This Pound Dad](https://thymosin-report.hbsnzj.com/blog/1748081263.html)

Reading his work crushed me. In his eyes, that was who I had become. And if my youngest son saw me that way, what were my two older boys seeing in me? What kind of example was I setting for them?

That moment hit me harder than any number on a scale ever could. It forced me to confront the reality that my habits were shaping how my children viewed me. If I didn’t change, I wasn’t just letting myself down. I was teaching them the wrong lessons about health, about discipline, about what it means to be a man and a father. It was the first day where I decided my story had to change.

I cleaned up my diet first, cutting out the junk. Then I focused my meals on lean protein, whole-food carbs, fruits, vegetables, and healthy fats. Making protein the foundation of every meal helped naturally control my hunger. Before, I’d never paid attention to calories, but I dramatically changed my portion sizes by weighing my food and tracking macros. I also made sure I was drinking plenty of water.

In terms of exercise, I didn’t try to overhaul everything at once. I started walking daily. Once I built momentum, I added resistance training. The workouts were tough at first, but feeling progress kept me motivated. I tried many different types of exercises before I fell in love with strength training. I dedicated several days a week to weight training. Ultimately, my weekly routine was a combination of weight training with 10,000 to 15,000 steps a day.

As my body changed, so did my mindset. I began to crave discipline. I realized that motivation comes and goes, but discipline stays. Consistency beats motivation every time. I learned that there are no shortcuts worth taking. And I learned that when you take control of your health, everything in your life improves: your confidence, your habits, your discipline, and your sense of purpose.

It took me about 18 months to lose about 115 pounds. This was all without using GLP-1 medications. I didn’t rush it, because I wanted the transformation to be sustainable long-term. My goal was to build a lifestyle I could maintain for the rest of my life. It wasn’t fast and it wasn’t easy, but it was real.

What truly kept me going was remembering my “why.” Why I started. For me, my “why” is my family, my future, and the man I wanted to become. I wanted to be a healthier and more present dad. I wanted to be the kind of husband my wife deserved. I also wanted to break old patterns, take control of my health, and become the type of leader who leads from the front, not from the sidelines.

[Year Old](https://regeneration-desk.hbsnzj.com/blog/0921534633.html)

Beyond the weight loss, I felt different in every area of my life. I felt sharper and more energetic, which translated to better business and an improved marriage. I grew more confident and my self-belief increased dramatically, which helped my mental health. I became a more present and active father. Most of all, I became someone who can keep a promise to himself to do better.

I’m nowhere near finished. Lifting weights and walking long distances have become major staples in my life but my ongoing goal is to continue to lean out and build muscle. Who knows—maybe someday I’ll try to get on a bodybuilding stage! It would be cool to have pictures of that for my grandkids and great-grandkids someday. Long-term, I want to stay consistent, stay healthy, and continue becoming the best version of myself.

[Check From His](https://insulin-pathways.hbsnzj.com/blog/5646295416.html)

If I could go back and tell myself anything, it would be this: you don’t need motivation. You need momentum. Once you see progress, even a little, it becomes addictive. The transformation you’re after isn’t just physical–it’s mental. The real win is who you become in the process.
