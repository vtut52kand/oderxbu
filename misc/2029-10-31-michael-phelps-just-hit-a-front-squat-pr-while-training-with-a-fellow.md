# Michael Phelps Just Hit a Front Squat PR While Training With a Fellow Olympian

[Michael Phelps Just](https://appetitecode.jcesqc8.com/)

You don't get to be one of the most decorated Olympic athlete of all time without putting in the work in the gym. Legendary swimmer Michael Phelps may have ended his professional career, but his training routine is still being emulated (to varying levels of success) by fitness influencers. In fact, prior to his retirement, Phelps' competition prep became such a topic of speculation that he had to go on the record and say that his infamous 12,000-calorie diet was not real.

In a new workout video that Phelps shared to Instagram, the legendary former swimmer—and most decorated Olympic athlete of all time—gave his followers a quick look at a milestone in his weights training. While being spotted by fellow swimmer and Team USA medalist Allison Schmitt, Phelps nailed a new single rep max of 240 pounds in the front squat, saying: "It's slowly coming back."

The squat has been one of Phelps' go-to strength-building moves for a long time: when Men's Health asked the legendary swimmer "deadlift or squat" back in 2016, he chose squat. However, the barbell front squat variation, which hits the glutes, hamstrings, hips and quadriceps, can present an altogether different challenge from the more common barbell back squat.

[Just Hit Front Squat](https://biopeptide.wiki/)

One of the aspects of the front squat that can make this move more challenging is the way one holds the bar. The traditional clean grip—as Phelps uses in the clip—can put the wrists in an uncomfortable overextended position, specifically if you struggle with mobility, which can lead to you having to bail on a set due to pain.

[Training With Fellow Olympian](https://peplawllc.com/)

One way to minimize this problem is to perform regular mobility exercises which flex, extend and rotate the wrist joints. Not only will this minimize pain and stiffness, it will also improve your range of motion when attempting the front squat. If those stretches don't work for you, you can switch to a bodybuilder grip, which allows you to rest the bar across your collarbone and shoulders and support it with your hands.
