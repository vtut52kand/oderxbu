Understanding Diabetic Neuropathy

Diabetic neuropathy is nerve damage caused by chronically high blood sugar levels. While it most commonly affects the feet and legs, it can also impact the hands and arms. Persistent high glucose injures tiny blood vessels that supply nerves, leading to pain, tingling, numbness, or weakness.

Key points:

Nerve damage develops gradually.

Both type 1 and type 2 diabetes can cause neuropathy.

Good blood sugar control reduces risk and slows progression.

Common Symptoms in the Hands

When neuropathy strikes your hands, you may experience a mix of uncomfortable sensations. Look out for:

Pins and needles (paresthesia): a prickling or tingling feeling

Numbness: reduced ability to feel temperature or pain

Burning or sharp pains: often worse at night

Weakness: difficulty gripping or performing fine motor tasks

Temperature sensitivity: hands feel unusually hot or cold

If you have persistent pins and needles in hands diabetes–related, it's important to address it early.

Diagnosis: What to Expect

A healthcare professional will usually start with a thorough history and physical exam. Diagnostic steps may include:

Detailed symptom review: onset, pattern, triggers

Neurological exam: checking strength, reflexes, and sensation

Monofilament test: assesses light touch sensation

Nerve conduction studies or electromyography (EMG) for severe cases

Blood tests: vitamin B12, thyroid function, kidney function

Early diagnosis allows for prompt intervention and better outcomes.

2. Medications for Nerve Pain

Several medications can help reduce neuropathic pain:

Antidepressants (e.g., duloxetine, amitriptyline)

Anti-seizure drugs (e.g., pregabalin, gabapentin)

Topical treatments (capsaicin cream, lidocaine patches)

Over-the-counter pain relievers (acetaminophen, NSAAIDs) – use sparingly

Always discuss side effects and potential interactions with your doctor.

3. Physical and Occupational Therapy

Preventing Complications

Unchecked neuropathy can lead to serious issues. To protect your hands:

Inspect hands daily for cuts, blisters, or infections

Keep nails trimmed and skin moisturized

Seek immediate care for wounds that don't heal

Adjust activities if you notice worsening numbness or pain

When to Seek Medical Attention

Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

Sudden loss of sensation or severe pain

Muscle weakness that limits daily activities

Signs of infection (redness, swelling, heat around a wound)

Any new or rapidly worsening symptoms
