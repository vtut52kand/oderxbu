# Yes, Underrated Spy Thrillers Without James Bond Do Exist

As one of the longest running film franchises ever, the British spy character James Bond has produced some incredible films with huge variety amongst themselves. Sure, you'll find outlandish gadgets and charming performances by one of the many silver screen actors, but there are far more cool spy movies outside of 007's exploits that deserve over twenty entries in a series. Spy movies can shed a new light on fascinating events from history—or just be a chance for actors and directors to go all out on wild action scene ideas—so we've accounted for both in this list.

"Confessions of a Dangerous Mind"

Confessions of a Dangerous Mind is the directorial debut of George Clooney, who steps into the role with confidence. The film fictionalizes the life of Chuck Barris, the game show creator and host who claimed to be an assassin for the CIA. Sam Rockwell absolutely steals the show with an unhinged turn as Barris, lending a bizarre feel to the whole production.

"The International"

German director Tom Tykwer did his own take on the spy thriller, with Clive Owen as an Interpol agent and Naomi Watts as an American Assistant District Attorney who are hunting evidence against an international bank suspected of money laundering, arms trading and more nefarious financial crimes. The photography and action is excellent—and Owen brings a ferocious determination to the lead role—though the plot can meander at points.

"Atomic Blonde"

Atomic Blonde is a neon-lit spy blockbuster set during the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989, with Charlize Theron playing an MI6 agent who has to track down a list of double agents in the field intercepted by the KGB. While the action is what we're mostly here for, there's also an intriguing Cold War mystery at play that itches your curiosity just enough to keep it lively.

"Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy"

Adapted from the classic John Le Carré novel, this is a much different take on the Cold War spy genre than Atomic Blonde, but just as great. It's got a treasure trove of great British actors including Gary Oldman, Mark Strong, Colin Firth, Benedict Cumberbatch, and Tom Hardy. Oldman plays Smiley, a British intelligence officer leading the investigation of a Soviet double agent among his high-ranking colleagues.

"Eraser"

This Schwarzenegger film has some fun fights and cool '90s tech gadgets, with the superstar playing a witness protection specialist. His goal is to protect a whistleblowing executive of a defense company that plans to sell its advanced weaponry on the black market.

"Fair Game"

This is an under-appreciated entry from Doug Liman, director of the excellent spy film The Bourne Identity. Naomi Watts stars as a CIA agent Valerie Plame, whose memoir provided the basis for this film. Plame's identity was leaked after her diplomat husband penned an op-ed critical of the George Bush administration during the 2003 Invasion of Iraq, and the film shows the leak's impact on her life and marriage.

This Hindi-language thriller is anything but underrated in India, where it debuted as the highest-grossing Indian film of 2023 so far, and it made a good splash overseas as well due to its slick action. If you haven't heard of it yet, it's actually the fourth movie in a shared universe of spy films and stars Shah Rukh Kanh as an exiled RAW agent who has to foil a deadly virus attack on India.

"A Most Wanted Man"

An international espionage film, A Most Wanted Man was one of the last film roles taken on by Phillip Seymour Hoffman, and he really slips into the role of German intelligence officer Günther Bachmann. It's a slow-burn buildup of tension as you learn about Bachmann's web of informants and suspects with him.

"BlackKKKlansman"

Spike Lee's biographical crime drama was blisteringly tense and bolstered by incredible performances from John David Washington and Adam Driver. They play detectives that work together to infiltrate a Colorado chapter of the Ku Klux Klan in the 1970s.

"Salt"

Angelina Jolie proves her action star chops as a CIA officer seeking to clear her name when an informant claims she is a Russian sleeper agent. She bring legitimate ambiguity and intrigue to the part, while also selling some cool fights and stunts.

"Clear and Present Danger"

This Jack Ryan thrill ride follows up from Patriot Games with Harrison Ford showing why he was, and still is, such a bankable lead. Willem DaFoe also shines as CIA operative John Clark in this story of a covert war against a Colombian drug cartel operating without people like Ryan—the newly-minted acting deputy director of the CIA—even being aware.
