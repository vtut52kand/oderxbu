What to Consider

Cushioning and Support

This is#1 for recovery shoes. Look for shoes with ample cushioning that will absorb shock and reduce impact on your joints. Though you won't be covering large distances in your recovery kicks, they should be supportive your foot type, too.

Breathability

After logging some miles laced-up tight in your favorite running sneakers, those feet are going to need a little fresh air. So opt for shoes that will let your feet breathe and cool down. Breathable materials like mesh or perforated leather are good choices to help keep your feet cool and dry, reducing the risk of blisters and other foot irritations.

Flexibility

The best recovery footwear is flexible enough to allow your feet to move naturally and without constraints. This can help improve circulation and reduce muscle soreness.

Style

There are a few different styles of recovery shoes out there, each with its own features and benefits. Consider whether you want an open-toe style like slides or flip flops. Or a closed-toe model like clogs or slip-ons, which can provide a little extra support and protection.

Size

A well-fitting pair of recovery shoes should have a roomy fit without being too loose. There should be about a thumb's width of space between your longest toe and the end of the shoe. Check the width, too. Make sure the shoe is wide enough to accommodate your foot without constricting it on the sides, midfoot, or heel, especially for non-sandal models.
