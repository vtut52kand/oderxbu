What Is Cortisol—and Why Nighttime Spikes Matter

Cortisol is produced by your adrenal glands, small organs sitting atop each kidney. Under normal conditions, cortisol:

Rises in the morning to help you wake up

Peaks around 8 a.m. to support alertness and metabolism

Gradually falls throughout the day

Reaches its lowest point in the late evening, allowing relaxation and deep sleep

A cortisol spike at night disrupts this cycle, leading to:

Poor sleep quality

Increased anxiety or restlessness

Impaired recovery and hormone balance

Over time, persistently high cortisol at night can affect health, mood, weight, and even immune function.

Common Cortisol Spike at Night Symptoms

Not everyone with elevated nighttime cortisol will experience all of these signs, but many people notice a combination of:

Difficulty falling asleep (sleep latency longer than 30 minutes)

Frequent night wakings or "light" sleep stages

Racing thoughts or an "overactive mind" when you try to doze off

Night sweats or feeling unusually warm in bed

Heart palpitations or a sense of internal jitteriness

Early morning awakenings (waking at 3–5 a.m.)

Persistent fatigue despite adequate time in bed

Unexplained weight gain, especially around the belly

Increased cravings for sugar or carbohydrates in the evening

If you tick several boxes on this list, high cortisol at night could be a factor.
