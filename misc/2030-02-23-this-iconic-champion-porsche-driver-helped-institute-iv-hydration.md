# ​​This Iconic Champion Porsche Driver Helped Institute IV Hydration

In the racing community, Hurley Haywood is a proper legend. It may be shorter to list famed events Haywood hasn’t won. He ascended to the top of the podium three times at the 24 Hours of Le Mans, twice at the 12 Hours of Sebring, five times at the Rolex 24 at Daytona, and took the 1988 Trans-Am title and two IMSA GT championships. The list goes on, but when the former Porsche factory driver put a wheel on a track, he typically dominated. (Patrick Dempsey, a race driver and team owner, is currently making a documentary about Haywood’s incredible career and life.)

Haywood knew he wanted to be a race car driver from a young age. However, the Vietnam War derailed his plans for a bit. “I was drafted in 1970,” Haywood tells Men’s Health, “and I had a very low number, so I was shipped overseas. I spent most of my tour telling everyone how I wanted to get back home and drive race cars.” Haywood says most of his fellow troops chuckled at what they surely thought was a pipe dream. But within a year of his return to the United States, Haywood won an IMSA GT title. “That was a great feeling,” Haywood grins.

A few years later, he’d go on to win the 24 Hours of Le Mans—arguably the most challenging endurance race—five times, starting in 1977, in Porsche’s 936. A slew of wins followed as his career continued well into the Nineties, with his final Le Mans victory coming in 1994. Now, Haywood works as the chief driving instructor for Porsche’s Sport Driving School. Each year, he decamps to Le Mans, to soak up the atmosphere. As the race gets underway and the field roars by, Haywood looks wistfully onto the circuit that’s so very entwined with his career. Inquire if he wishes he was out there again and Haywood’s quick to say no; that racing’s changed so much over the years, it wouldn’t be the same. “But I do miss the competition,” he concludes. “There’s nothing like digging in when you really had to and coming out on top.”

Aside from a lasting legacy that is forever etched in the racing record books, Haywood also left motorsport a little better than he found it. One fascinating contribution was when his approach to hydration. During a stint in the car, where temperatures can soar well into the 100s, dehydration from sweating is a common issue plaguing drivers. Drivers can sweat so much, they actually rust their car.
