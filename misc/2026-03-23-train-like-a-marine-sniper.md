For Marine Corps Scout Snipers, the transformation process is the same. Scout snipers go through an intense training period where they begin as Professionally Instructed Gunmen, or PIGs, and graduate as Hunters of Gunmen (HOGs). HOGs are experts in stealth and reconnaissance for deployment deep behind enemy lines. They're trained to hit precise targets at incredible distances--like shooting to kill from half a mile away. But whereas eating more is the only thing pigs have to do become hogs, it doesn't come quite as easily for these PIGs

In order to join the ranks of the less-than-300 active snipers in the entire Marine Corps, as shown in the latest episode of Discovery's Surviving the Cut, 32 PIGs must endure a 9-week reconnaissance course at Camp Pendleton, California. Having already passed strict requirements for fitness, marksmanship, and overall excellence, they're now faced with a wrenching course of endless physical conditioning, all while mastering the precision skills of a sniper, and learning to be among the sneakiest and stealthiest men on the planet.

PIGs must literally become pigs, crawling through mud and brush in ghillie suits (camouflage suits with leaves, sticks, and other scraps of foliage designed to blend in with natural surroundings) to do whatever it takes to remain undetected and make their shots. In battle, if snipers are spotted, they're dead.

When they're not practicing stalking, they're putting their marksmanship skills to the test. In the "Unknown Distance Qualification" challenge, each sniper fires 35 shots at stationary and moving targets up to 800 yards away. It takes tremendous focus and a keen understanding of environmental conditions that can affect a bullet's path--wind, temperature, and humidity--to hit the targets. Above all, snipers must have excellent vision to see such small targets from long distances.

You can train your eyes to see like a sniper. All it takes is a healthy dose of blinking and B vitamins. "Take 'blinking breaks' throughout the day--blinks work like windshield wipers, clearing up the surface of the eye and encouraging tear production," says Ernest Kornmehl, M.D., a professor of ophthalmology at Harvard medical school. Train yourself to blink every time you perform a frequent action, such as clicking your mouse or flicking your turn signal.

Also, USDA research found that people with high intakes of the B vitamins riboflavin and thiamin had the least clouding of their eyes' lenses, the most direct measure of cataract risk. Increase your intake with a daily multivitamin containing your quota of B1 (thiamin) and B2 (riboflavin). And if you miss a day, grab some trail mix that contains nuts, seeds, and M&Ms to replenish both vitamins.
