When should I seek medical attention for chest pain on the left side?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Left-side chest pain requires prompt medical attention, particularly when it is new, severe, or accompanied by warning signs such as shortness of breath, pain radiating to the arm or jaw, sweating, nausea, or dizziness. These symptoms may indicate a heart attack or another serious cardiovascular condition and should be treated as a medical emergency. Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room immediately.

Explanation

If you experience chest pain on the left side, it's important to pay attention to your body. You should call emergency services or go to the hospital right away if the pain is sudden, severe, and accompanied by other worrying symptoms like shortness of breath, nausea, sweating, or pain spreading to your arm, neck, or jaw. These symptoms may be signs of a heart attack or other serious heart problems. Even if the pain is not severe, if it lasts longer than a few minutes, keeps coming back, or if you have a history of heart disease, it's best to have it checked by a doctor. Sometimes chest pain may be due to other causes such as muscle strain or acid reflux, but because heart problems can be life-threatening, it's better to be cautious. If you're unsure about your symptoms or want to better understand what might be causing your chest pain, you can use a free symptom checker to help identify possible conditions before speaking with your doctor. Your doctor can ask you questions, perform tests, and determine if the pain is related to your heart, lungs, or another part of your body. Getting professional help ensures you receive the proper care to keep you safe and well.
