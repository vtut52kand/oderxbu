# Vitamin D Deficit Can Be Deadly

Insufficient levels of milk’s favorite vitamin may be joining smoking, obesity, diabetes, and high blood pressure as a cause of early death, according to a recent Johns Hopkins study.

Researchers found that people with deficient levels of vitamin D had a 26 percent increased risk of death—usually ending in heart attack or heart diseases, though researchers said numbers were not high enough to make a link between low vitamin D levels and heart attacks.

A separate study found that about 41 percent of American men are estimated to be vitamin D deficient.

How do you know if you’re low on this wonder vitamin? Michos says the only way to know for sure is to get screened. If your blood work shows insufficient levels, a physician will prescribe the right dose of the supplement to get you back in the normal range.

You can boost your D naturally by eating a diet rich in fish­—including tuna, salmon, sardines, and mackerel—consuming fortified dairy products, taking vitamin supplements, and enjoying brief exposure to the sun. But Michos warns it’s difficult to get enough vitamin D from your diet alone, especially if you’re starting at a deficit.

“It’ll take more than milk to get it back to a normal range,” she says.
