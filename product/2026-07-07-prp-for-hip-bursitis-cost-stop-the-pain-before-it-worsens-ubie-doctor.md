What Is Hip Bursitis?

Bursae are small sacs filled with fluid that reduce friction between bones, tendons, and muscles. In the hip, the trochanteric bursa (on the outer side) is most often affected.

Common symptoms include:

Outer hip pain that worsens with activity or lying on the affected side

Tenderness to touch

Stiffness, especially after resting

Pain that radiates down the thigh

Left untreated, hip bursitis can limit mobility and quality of life. Treatment often begins with conservative measures such as rest, ice, anti-inflammatory medications, and physical therapy.

What Is PRP Therapy?

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses a concentrated sample of your own blood platelets—cells that release growth factors to aid tissue repair. The process involves:

Drawing a small blood sample

Spinning it in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets

Injecting the platelet-rich portion into the injured bursa

Potential benefits of PRP for hip bursitis:

Accelerated tissue healing

Reduced inflammation

Decreased pain

Minimal risk of allergic reaction (uses your own blood)

Clinical studies on hip bursitis are still limited, but evidence from related joint and tendon treatments is promising.

Factors That Influence PRP Cost

Several variables affect the final price you pay:

• Clinic location: Urban centers often charge more than rural areas.• Provider expertise: Board-certified sports medicine physicians or orthopedic surgeons may have higher fees.• Type of centrifuge system: Automated systems that produce higher platelet concentrations can add cost.• Number of injections: Chronic pain may require a series of injections.• Additional imaging: Ultrasound guidance during injection can improve accuracy but adds fees.

Always ask for a detailed breakdown so you understand what you're paying for.

Insurance Coverage & Out-of-Pocket Expenses

Most insurance plans classify PRP therapy as experimental or investigational for hip bursitis, meaning it's often not covered. However:

Some carriers may cover portions of the cost if PRP is deemed "medically necessary"

Medicare and Medicaid generally do not cover PRP for bursitis

Flexible Spending Accounts (FSAs) or Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) may be used to offset out-of-pocket expenses

Before scheduling, check with your insurance provider and billing office:

Is PRP covered under your plan?

Will you need pre-authorization?

Can you submit a claim for reimbursement?

Alternative and Complementary Therapies

While PRP may offer faster healing, other options can be helpful alone or in combination:

Dry needling or acupuncture: May alleviate pain and improve circulation

Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT): High-energy sound waves to reduce inflammation

Orthobiologics: Stem cell injections (experimental and costly)

Lifestyle changes: Weight management, low-impact exercises (swimming, cycling)

Discuss all possibilities with your healthcare team to choose the best plan.

When to Seek Immediate Care

Hip bursitis is rarely life-threatening, but certain signs warrant urgent evaluation:

Severe swelling or redness over the hip

Fever above 100.4°F (38°C)

Sudden inability to move the hip or bear weight

Intense, worsening pain despite rest

If you experience any of these, contact your doctor or visit the nearest emergency department.
