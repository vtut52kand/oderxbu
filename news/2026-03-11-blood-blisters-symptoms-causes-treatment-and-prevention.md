Signs and Symptoms of a Blood Blister

[2]

A blood blister changes in appearance over time. “When the blood blister is fresh [or recent], the blood is redder in color because it’s also fresh,” explains Anna Chacon, MD, a dermatologist based in Miami.

As blood trapped under the skin ages, the blister darkens, turning dark red, purple, or even black, says Dr. Chacon. This color change is part of the healing process as the body reabsorbs the blood.

Blood blisters can also vary in size, with some causing itching. The affected area can feel warm or sore to the touch, notes Chacon.

How Is a Blood Blister Diagnosed?

There’s no specific test to diagnose a blood blister — doctors can identify it through a visual examination.

Your doctor may take a fluid sample from the blood blister if there’s concern of an infection or other serious cause, notes Mary Alice Mina, MD, a dermatologist at Baucom & Mina Derm Surgery in Atlanta.

“If a blood blister forms in an unusual area, such as around the eyes, mouth, or genital region, a fluid sample can help rule out more serious conditions,” explains Kopelman.

For example, a fluid sample from a blood blister in the mouth may be tested for signs of oral cancer or infections. For blood blisters in the eyes or genital region, a sample can help identify underlying skin conditions, autoimmune disorders, or infections that contributed to the blister’s formation, Kopelman says.

Prevention of Blood Blisters

You can’t always prevent injury, but understanding the risk factors for blood blisters and taking necessary precautions can help reduce their occurrence.

Wear properly fitted shoes to avoid pinching and pressure on your feet.

Use protective gloves when working with tools or heavy objects.

Add padding inside your shoes if you feel pressure in specific areas.

Be careful when opening drawers and heavy doors and drawers to prevent fingers from getting caught.

Complications of Blood Blisters

While blood blisters usually heal on their own, some can become infected if bacteria enters through a break in the skin.

The Takeaway

Blood blisters form when blood vessels under the skin break, filling the pocket of skin with blood. While they often resolve without treatment, complications like infection can occur, causing redness, swelling, or pus. Seek medical attention if you notice signs of infection, the blister doesn’t improve, or the blister develops on your eye, mouth, or genitals.
