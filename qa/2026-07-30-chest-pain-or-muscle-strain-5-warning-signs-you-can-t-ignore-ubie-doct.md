How can I tell if my chest pain is due to muscle strain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Chest pain that worsens when you move, breathe deeply, or press on the area—especially after heavy exercise or an injury—is often caused by a muscle strain. This type of pain is typically localized, reproducible with touch, and tends to improve with rest, ice, and over-the-counter pain relievers.

Explanation

Muscle strain in the chest often shows up as pain in a specific spot. One way to tell if the pain is due to muscle strain is to see if you can make the pain worse by moving your upper body, stretching, or pressing on the area where you feel discomfort.

For example, if the pain started after you lifted something heavy, did a lot of exercise, or had a minor injury, and you feel more pain when you take a deep breath or when someone presses on the sore spot, it is likely to be from the muscles or the chest wall rather than the heart.
