40s, Male

I am experiencing a slight stabbing, needle-like pain roughly 2-3 inches below my left armpit. I would like to know if it is possible for gas to cause this type of pain. I have been worried about heart trouble, but I do have gastrointestinal symptoms regularly, and my late mother was diagnosed with stomach ulcers. Given these symptoms and my history, what are the possible conditions?

Dr. Taylor’s Response

I understand your concern about the pain under your left armpit, especially given your family history and worry about heart issues. While I can't provide a diagnosis, I can share some general information.

In my experience, pain in this area can indeed be related to gas or other gastrointestinal issues. The stomach and parts of the large intestine are located in the upper left abdomen, and gas or inflammation in these areas can sometimes cause referred pain that's felt higher up, even near the armpit.

[Below Left Armpit GI](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/blog/6585725870.html)

However, this location is also close to other structures like ribs, muscles, and lymph nodes. Possible causes could include:

Gastric issues (like gas or ulcers)

Muscular strain

Costochondritis (inflammation of rib cartilage)

Lymph node swelling

[Possible Causes](https://pigmentlab.jcesqc8.com/)

While heart-related pain is possible, it's less common to feel it only in this specific area.

Given your symptoms and family history, I strongly recommend seeing your primary care doctor. They can perform a physical exam, review your medical history, and order any necessary tests to determine the cause of your pain.
