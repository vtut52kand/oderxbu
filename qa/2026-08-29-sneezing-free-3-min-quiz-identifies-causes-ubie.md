About the Symptom

A sneeze is a sudden, forceful, uncontrolled burst of air through the nose and mouth. It is most often due some irritant in the nose or allergic response.

Possible Causes

Generally, Sneezing can be related to:

A condition where temperature changes cause symptoms like runny nose, blocked nose, and sneezing.

Also known as rebound rhinitis or rhinitis medicamentosa. This condition is caused by overuse of nose-drops containing oxymetazoline, which are often prescribed for stuffy nose. If used for too long, the nose becomes "dependent" on the nose-drops and becomes congested once the nose-drops are stopped.

A condition caused by allergens like pollen and dust mites, resulting in a runny nose, stuffy nose, sneezing, and itchy eyes.

Sinusitis

Acute Tonsillitis / Pharyngitis

Influenza (Flu)

Common Cold (Upper Respiratory Tract Infection)

Cough Variant Asthma

Related Serious Diseases

Sometimes, Sneezing may be related to these serious diseases:

A condition where the airways are highly sensitive and overreact to foreign substances or environmental changes. The underlying cause is unclear but involves genetics. Many triggers for asthma flares include smoke, pollen, dust mite droppings, cold weather, exercise, and viral infections.

Pneumonia (Lower Respiratory Tract Infection)

Doctor's Diagnostic Questions

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this symptom:

Have you been sneezing frequently?

How often do you experience sneezing?

Regarding your sneezing, how many times do you experience it each day?

How is your sneezing now?
