# What's the Best Type of Gym Shoe? Fitness Experts Break Down What You Need for Most Workouts.

IF YOU'RE LOOKING to maximize your gym gains, picking the right pair of shoes can be a major factor between a good workout and a great one. There’s no shortage of training sneaker options—so it helps to understand exactly what you can (and should) expect from your choice of footwear.

Every gym-goer trains differently, so you might have to trial-and-error a few different styles until you find your Cinderella fit. To help you understand the shoe landscape, exercise physiologist and strength coach Dr. Pat Davidson and MH fitness director Ebenezer Samuel, C.S.C.S. have put together some general guidelines to help you make an informed choice. Plus, they discuss how to test if a shoe is worth buying and their favorite recommendations.

If you’re looking to squat better, Davidson says heel elevation is a legit gym hack. The stability of the built-in heel helps you to get into the position more effectively with an upright torso, allowing for better activation of quads and glutes. “You’re going to be able to squat ten times better mechanically compared to not having it,” he explains. Some people elevate their heels using plates or wedges—but weightlifting shoes (as in, the category of shoe) have built-in wedges in the heel to provide that lift, along with stability features like straps to help improve lockdown.

Workouts that feature heavy deadlifts and other hinge movements, on the other hand, will require different footwear. Instead, Samuel says to think about putting your heel in position to drive into the ground and create force. This means you’ll want a flat shoe where your heel is as close to the ground as possible. “If I’m in anything except the Vivobarefoot, my deadlift goes down by 20 or 30 pounds,” Samuel says. “Just because of that little millimeter.”

If you’re on a wooden floor, Davidson recommends going barefoot or wearing socks on a rubber mat. “Your connection with the floor is everything for lower body lifts,” he adds. But when it comes to upper body lifts—whether it’s bench pressing or using machines—Samuel and Davidson both agree there’s no specific shoe to wear for a performance boost.

Davidson's general tip for spotting junk shoes is to crush the heel. He uses his fingers to crush the back of the shoe—and if it crushes, it likely won’t provide lasting support. Davidson also bends the bottom to see if the shoe has a solid midsole. “I want it to be bending out at the ball of the foot because that's gonna sort of go along with the design of the human foot more in general,” he adds.

The structure of feet varies and can narrow down your choices. Davidson, for example, has a wide toe box so he can only fit into certain models, like the Under Armour Tribase Reign and the Nike Monarch—popularly known as the daddest of dad shoes. “You can stand in them all day, and they're comfortable and super supportive and wide. Literally, they built it to be the dad grilling shoe.”
