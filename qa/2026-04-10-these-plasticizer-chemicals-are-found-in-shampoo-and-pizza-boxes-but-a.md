# These ‘Plasticizer’ Chemicals Are Found in Shampoo and Pizza Boxes. But Are They Harmful to Your Health?

PHTHALATES ARE OFTEN referred to as the “everywhere chemicals.” That’s because they’re found in so many different products that you probably use every day.

[But Are They Harmful](https://reboundworks.escortskhi.com/blog/2065772596.html)

“Everything from vinyl flooring and window coverings, to medical devices like IV tubing and blood bags, to perfumes and other personal care products,” says Stephanie Engel, Ph.D., professor of epidemiology and deputy director of the Center for Environmental Health and Susceptibility at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

Phthalates (pronounced thal-eights) are a group of chemicals that are added to products for several reasons, such as to make plastics soft and flexible or to bind scents to detergent, she says. However, these chemicals can leach out, potentially harming your health.

“There is a large body of evidence linking phthalate exposure to negative health impacts,” says Engel, who has researched some of these effects. Here’s what to know about them.

What are phthalates?

[They Harmful](https://incretin-path.escortskhi.com/blog/1161197183.html)

PHTHALATES ARE A family of several chemicals that have been used in many consumer products since the 1930s, according to the Natural Resources Defense Council (NRDC).

They’re found in personal care items including shampoo or lotion, food containers, and plastics, says Mahua Choudhury, Ph.D., associate professor of pharmaceutical sciences at Texas A&M University.

“It’s not only plastic, like a water bottle,” she explains. “It can be in a pizza box, carpet, or perfume.”

People are exposed to phthalates by ingesting them from food, absorbing them through the skin, or breathing in dust containing them, NRDC says. Engel says diet is likely a “major source of human exposure” in the U.S.

“A number of phthalates are approved for use in food packaging and food contact materials, and phthalates have been measured in finished food products,” she says. This is mostly true for highly processed and fast foods.

Are they harmful to your health?

DECADES OF RESEARCH have shown that phthalates can have an impact on your health in a number of ways.

A recent report published in the journal Lancet eBioMedicine found that daily exposure to phthalates may be linked to more than 356,000 global heart disease deaths in 2018 alone.

While cardiovascular disease has long been linked to diet, physical activity, and smoking, the research suggests that now, “plastics are a major risk factor to be considered alongside these other exposures,” says Leonardo Trasande, M.D., a pediatrics professor and director of the New York University Center for the Investigation of Environmental Hazards, who co-authored the study.

Previous research has shown that phthalate exposure can cause inflammation in the body, which raises the risk of heart attack and stroke. Phthalates have also been linked to diabetes and obesity, Choudhury says.

The chemicals are known to affect testosterone levels and male reproductive system development, as well, Engel says. And, they’re connected with an increased risk of preterm births and affect childhood development.

Engel led research published in the American Journal of Public Health in 2021 that showed phthalates could increase children’s risk for learning, attention and behavioral disorders.

Why exactly the chemicals affect the body hasn’t been clearly established, Engel says. It’s likely due to phthalates disrupting the body’s hormonal systems, including sex and thyroid hormones. They’re also linked to inflammation and oxidative stress, which are connected to several poor health outcomes.

How to Minimize Exposure

CHOUDHURY SAYS PEOPLE often ask her the question of how to reduce exposure, and it’s difficult to answer. Phthalates can be tough to avoid since they’re in so many everyday products. It’s also sometimes impossible to tell if something contains a phthalate.

For instance, Engel says scent formulations are proprietary, and fragrances may contain hundreds of ingredients that aren’t always listed.

NRDC suggests looking for certain acronyms on products that suggest a phthalate is included:

BBP (Butyl benzyl phthalate)

DBP (Dibutyl phthalate), often found in nail polish

DEHP (Di-(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate), often in medical products, like disposable gloves, tubes, catheters, and blood bags

DEP (Diethyl phthalate), often in personal care products

DiDP (Di-isodecyl phthalate)

DiNP (Di-isonoyl phthalate), often used in toys and childcare products

DnHP (di-n-hexyl phthalate)
