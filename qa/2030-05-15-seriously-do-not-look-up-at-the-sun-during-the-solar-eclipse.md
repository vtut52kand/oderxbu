# Seriously, Do Not Look Up At the Sun During the Solar Eclipse

On August 21, a total solar eclipse will cross the country for the first time since 1918. Since it won’t happen again anytime soon, it’s not surprising that people want to get out and take a look.

[Seriously Do Not Look](https://mendstride.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/3991914536.html)

But unless you have already purchased special solar eclipse glasses, you definitely should not go outside and look at the sun today. If you do, you can cause serious, lifelong damage to your eyes—and you may not even know it until later on.

That’s what happened to Louis Tomososki, who was 16 years old when a partial solar eclipse passed through Oregon back in 1963. He watched it unprotected in the baseball fields behind his high school, closing his left eye and leaving his right open, he told Fox 12 Oregon.

[Seriously Do Not](https://peplawllc.com/)

He looked at it for just 20 seconds, he estimates. But that was enough time to permanently damage his retina, leaving him with a sizable blind spot in the one eye—similar to looking at a person’s face, but not being able to see their nose. (These are the 8 worst things you can do to your eyes.)

And Tomososki’s was not an isolated case. During an eclipse, the lenses of your eyes can concentrate the rays of the sun so strongly to cause an immediate burn in your retina, essentially causing a hole in your vision, ophthalmologist Russell Van Gelder, M.D., Ph.D., explains in the American Academy of Ophthalmology’s video on solar eclipse safety.

This is referred to as solar retinopathy and eclipse retinopathy, explained Asim Farooq, M.D., an assistant professor of ophthalmology and visual science at UChicago Medicine to Science Life. Damage—which can be permanent—can occur in a minute, and it doesn’t need to be continuous, either. That means staring at the sun for just a few seconds at time can add up. Plus, since the sky is much darker than usual during an eclipse, we may not blink or look away as reflexively as we’d do other times during a sunny day.
