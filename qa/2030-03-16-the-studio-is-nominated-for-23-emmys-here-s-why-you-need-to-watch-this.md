# 'The Studio' Is Nominated for 23 Emmys—Here's Why You Need to Watch This Industry Favorite

THE TV LANDSCAPE is crowded these days. There are a lot of shows, and, believe it or not, most people don't have enough free time to watch everything. In fact, the average TV viewer will probably watch a mere fraction of even the great stuff that's out there. We have to choose what gets our attention wisely.

That's why it's extra important to note a particularly special show when one comes along—and in 2025, The Studio is that show. Created by the Superbad power duo of Seth Rogen and Evan Goldberg, The Studio follows a movie executive named Matt Remick (Rogen) who is named head of Continental, one of the film industry's most influential and historical studios—but then has to fight the constant battle of making movies that will make a lot of money, and making movies that are, you know, good.

This might sound a little bit inside baseball for people deeply into the film industry, but let us assure you: There's something here for everyone. The movie stuff is simply a backdrop for the show's eight episodes of nonstop comedic chaos, as Matt, along with his valued colleagues (played by a standout cast that includes Ike Barinholtz, Catherine O'Hara, Chase Sui Wonders, and Kathryn Hahn), get into a different frenzied situation in each 30-minute episode. The fast-paced madness is often set to single-take shots and a jazz-percussion soundtrack, as Matt neurotically stumbles his way into and out of trouble; think of The Studio like if Curb Your Enthusiasm met Whiplash and Birdman.

[Studio' Is](https://tiancansz.com/2ezm37ie/)

The show shares more than just Curb Your Enthusiasm's brand of humor, too—it's also filled with all sorts of delightful guest stars. Some, like Bryan Cranston (who plays new Continental CEO Griffin Mill) are taking on characters, but many—including the likes of Martin Scorsese, Ron Howard, Zoë Kravitz, and Anthony Mackie, all Emmy-nominated for their work—are playing hilarious and self-deprecating versions of themselves. The Studio is a love-letter to movies and a real treat for true buffs, but it's also a fast-paced, hyper-frenetic comedy series that anyone who loves to laugh will really value. It's funny, yes, but it's also well-made, right on the level of the many differing great movies (one episode is a noir; another focuses on a 'Oner' take) it plays tribute to.

The Studio is set to be a major player at the 2025 Primetime Emmys, as it's nominated for 23 awards—tying the record set a couple years ago by FX's The Bear. The show has already won nine awards (including Best Guest Actor for Bryan Cranston) after the Creative Arts Emmys, which were held on September 6. If you want to be part of it (and if you want to get in on one of TV's greatest new shows), then hop right aboard. As the show makes abundantly clear, Continental Studios is always looking for more support.

The Player was an obvious inspiration for The Studio, down to that executive (as played by Tim Robbins) being named Griffin Mill—the same name as the CEO character played by Bryan Cranston in The Studio. At the very least, The Studio plays direct homage to The Player, but you could also consider a world where these two stories are directly connected.

The Player is one of the best movies of Altman's legendary career, and is based on the novel of the same name by Michael Tolkin. It's a must-watch movie for anyone, but especially if you're a big fan of movies or a new fan of The Studio.
