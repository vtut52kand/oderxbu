The "Too Much" Trap: Understanding Biphasic Dose Response in Red Light Therapy

Red light therapy (RLT) has surged in popularity for skin rejuvenation, pain relief and overall wellness. Yet, as with many treatments, more isn't always better. The concept of a biphasic dose response teaches us that both too little and too much light can limit benefits—or even cause setbacks. Here's what you need to know to hit the sweet spot.

What Is a Biphasic Dose Response?

A biphasic dose response describes how a substance (or treatment) produces beneficial effects at lower to moderate doses, but diminished or harmful effects at higher doses. Key points:

Stimulation Phase – Low-to-moderate doses trigger positive cellular responses.

Inhibition Phase – Higher doses suppress or damage those same responses.

Also called a "hormetic curve" or "Arndt-Schulz effect" in broader pharmacology.

Applies to many therapies and nutrients—red light therapy is no exception.

Why Biphasic Dose Response Matters in Red Light Therapy

Red light therapy relies on specific wavelengths of red and near-infrared light (600–1000 nm) to stimulate cellular energy production, reduce inflammation, and support tissue repair. Overexposure can:

Generate excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS)

Overheat skin or underlying tissues

Trigger cellular stress pathways instead of healing pathways

Understanding the biphasic response helps you:

Maximize benefits

Minimize wasted time

The Science Behind Biphasic Response in Red Light Therapy

Moderate red/NIR doses boost cytochrome c oxidase activity.

Cells produce more ATP (energy), supporting repair and function.

Low-to-moderate ROS serve as signaling molecules for healing.

Finding the Sweet Spot: Dosing Guidelines

While individual needs vary depending on skin type, condition, and device, these general parameters help you avoid the "too much" trap:

Red: 630–660 nm

Near-Infrared (NIR): 810–850 nm

Therapeutic window: 4–10 J/cm² per session

Below 4 J/cm² may be subclinical (minimal benefit)

Above 10 J/cm² increases risk of inhibitory effects

20–100 mW/cm² is typical

Higher irradiance shortens session time but may risk heat buildup

5–15 minutes per area, depending on device strength

2–5 times per week for acute issues (e.g., injury)

1–3 times per week for maintenance and cosmetic goals

Signs You've Overdone It

Even though red light therapy is generally safe, watch for these mild warning signs of excessive dosing:

Temporary redness or warmth that lasts hours

Increased sensitivity or mild stinging

Headache or eye strain (if protective eyewear isn't used)

Plateau or return of symptoms you were targeting

If you notice persistent discomfort, scale back dose or frequency, and allow tissues to recover.

Tips to Avoid the "Too Much" Trap

Read your device manual carefully—note recommended distance and times.

Track your sessions: dose, duration and any effects.

Give yourself rest days. Even beneficial therapies need recovery cycles.

Be consistent: small, regular doses often outperform sporadic high doses.

When to Seek Professional Guidance

Red light therapy is low-risk, but if you have serious or persistent symptoms—especially involving pain, swelling, breathing difficulties or unexplained weight changes—don't rely solely on self-treatment. Biphasic dose response means "more" isn't always "better."
